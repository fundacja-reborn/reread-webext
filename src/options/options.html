First steps

Nothing translates yet - the model that would do it is not yet on this device. Setting up takes a minute:

  • Download a translation model Translation models
  • Download a dictionary (optional) Dictionaries
  • Pin re/read to the toolbar

Last, pin re/read to the toolbar: the puzzle-piece icon at the right end of the bar, then the gear next to re/read and "Pin to Toolbar". The button opens the reader, the reading list, your saved phrases and the switch for the site you are on.

Languages

Shows the language pairs downloaded to this device. To add new languages, download them in the Translation models section below.

Without a translation model, the pair says which language you are reading and where saved phrases are kept. Pairs come from installed models and dictionaries.

Translation models

Engine: Bergamot, inside the package - translates offline

Download a translation model here for each language pair you want to translate. These are official Mozilla files that run entirely offline on your device.

A model translates sentences well, but a single word on its own is often translated wrongly - the result is cut short, or is a word that does not exist. For single words, a dictionary from the section below is the better source.

List from -

Model downloads come from - - where Mozilla publishes them. The extension connects to this public server only when you press Update the list or Download. No personal data is ever sent.

Add a model from files

To add by hand a model the list above does not offer, load its files here.

Pick the three files of one direction and they are stored locally. Gzipped files are unpacked here, so the downloads can be used as they come:

  • model.enpl.intgemm.alphas.bin
  • lex.50.50.enpl.s2t.bin
  • vocab.enpl.spm

Adding a model by hand makes no network request, and this page stays the way in on the day the place models are downloaded from stops answering.

Dictionaries only, without machine translation; the reader, the offline reading list and the highlighter keep working.

With a language pair chosen, the bubble still shows dictionary meanings, and you can save phrases and see their underlines. Ideal for reading with dictionaries or in your native language. Your saved data is not deleted and returns when you turn this off.

With this on, selecting text on an ordinary page does nothing at all - open a page in the reader from re/read's button or with Alt+Shift+R.

In the reader a selection is only a highlight for the moment: no bubble opens, a tap or Escape clears it, and Ctrl+C or Cmd+C copies it (on a touch screen nothing can be copied).

Dictionaries

Add dictionaries to see detailed word definitions in the translation bubble. You can change the order in which they appear using the arrows.

The dictionaries listed below come from WikDict. You can also add your own - from files or from a link, and several for one language - in the sections under the list. Where to find them, each with its licence: reapps.eu/read.

List from -

The bubble answers in the order below. Only the dictionaries of the language you are reading answer.

    Dictionary downloads come from - - where WikDict publishes the dictionaries it builds from Wiktionary (CC BY-SA). The extension connects to this public server only when you press Update the list or Download. No personal data is ever sent.

    Add a dictionary from files

    Import your own dictionaries in the StarDict format. Unpack the archive, select the required files (.ifo, .idx, .dict), choose the languages and press Add dictionary.

    • name.ifo - what the dictionary calls itself
    • name.idx - the index of words
    • name.dict or name.dict.dz - the entries
    • name.syn - other spellings, when the dictionary has them

    A monolingual dictionary works too, for example English-English: choose the same language on both sides.

    Adding from files makes no network request. Any StarDict files work, including the catalogue's own, which are published at download.wikdict.com/dictionaries/stardict.

    Add a dictionary from a link

    Paste the address of a StarDict dictionary packed as a .zip file, choose the languages and press Download. The extension connects to that address once, when you press the button, and to nowhere else; nothing of yours is sent along. The archive is unpacked and checked on this device, exactly like files you pick yourself.

    Where to find dictionaries, monolingual ones too - English-English, for example - each with its licence: reapps.eu/read.

    Only https addresses are accepted. An archive that is not a .zip file (.tar.xz, .tar.zst) has to be unpacked and added from files.

    Where it works

    Underlines and the bubble only in the reader; on an ordinary page a selection shows a button that opens it there.

    With it on, no web page can tell which of its words you have saved. On Android and iOS it is on by default.

    Switched-off sites

    A list of websites where the extension is switched off: no translation, no bubble, no underlines. Add a site with the switch under the extension's icon in the browser's toolbar while you are on it, or type its address below. There is no switch when both Use without a translation model and No bubble when selecting text are on: nothing happens on ordinary pages then, so there is nothing to switch off.

    The address as the browser shows it, for example example.org; www.example.org is the same site. A subdomain such as news.example.org is a separate site. A page's whole address can be pasted; only the site part is kept.

    Bubble and phrases

    Bubble

    The bubble shows the translated sentence and the dictionary entries right away, with no press on More.

    Worth keeping on with a dictionary installed - a translation model often gets a single word wrong, and the dictionary shows what it really means. The bubble over an already saved phrase shows only the saved translation - the sentence and the dictionary entries appear after you press "More".

    The bubble shows the translation alone; the buttons appear when you click or tap it.

    The action buttons are copy, save, read aloud and the rest - everything the bubble can do, one press away.

    Bubble text size

    100%

    Adjusts the text and button size inside the translation bubble across all pages.

    Saved phrases

    Saving a phrase saves the sentence it stood in as well.

    The sentence as the page shows it, without its translation. It is shown under the phrase on the saved phrases page, folded to one line, and written into the third column of the export for Anki. For a phrase saved without a sentence, the sentence around it is saved the next time you open its bubble on a page. Like the phrase, the sentence is stored only on this device. Switched off, no new sentence is saved; the ones already saved are not deleted.

    Save "read", and "reads" and "reading" are underlined too.

    The bubble over them shows what you saved for "read". Which forms count is decided by your dictionary for the language you read, so one has to be installed. For now only when you read English. Switched off, only the exact form you saved is underlined.

    Reading view

    Every page you open in the reader goes into the offline reading list.

    Your saved pages, highlights and reading progress are kept safe even if the original website changes or disappears.

    Saved

    A typeface installed on this device, for example Noto Serif; it then appears as Custom in the Type row of the Aa panel.

    Type the name as the system spells it. Leave the field empty to use the built-in typefaces only.

    words a minute Saved

    Used only to estimate the reading time shown in the reading list and under an article's title.

    The number of words you read in a minute. 200 is an unhurried pace in your own language; in a language you are learning it is usually 100-150.

    Pages layout

    These settings work in the reader's Pages layout, where the text moves only by whole pages, each beginning and ending with a whole line.

    A page count at the foot of the window.

    Off, the last line of each page stands lower; the count is still read by screen readers, and on a wide screen it stands in the bar next to re/read.

    How a finger turns the pages.

    On a wide screen the default is a tap, on a narrow one a slide - there the hand that holds the device rests on the screen, and a gesture that has to move is the one it cannot make by accident. The page keys, the arrow keys and the mouse wheel turn pages whatever this is set to, and the pen never turns one.

    A sign that the page has turned.

    It is easy to miss when you are reading the middle of a page. Nothing happens if your system asks for less motion, and nothing when the page is turned by reading aloud or by dragging a selection to the edge of the page.

    Reading aloud

    The read-aloud buttons in the bubble, in the lists and in the reader.

    Turning this off hides every one of them. The voice and the speed you chose stay stored and come back when you turn it on again.

    A separate voice for each language; only voices that speak offline are offered.

    The voice reads phrases and whole articles alike. Speech is made on this device and nothing is sent to the internet.

    Data and copies

    Data on this device

    Disk space used by the extension.

    Dictionaries take up more space than their file size because of the database structure. Deleting an article or book completely removes its text, images, highlights, notes and reading progress. The rest - the dictionaries, the reading list, your phrases and the highlights - is not broken down: a dictionary costs several times what its text weighs, and counting the rest would mean reading every row of the databases each time this page opens.

    • Translation models

    A second copy of the reading list, safe from the browser's own clean-ups; it takes twice the space.

    It survives a database error too: your saved articles are kept even if the database is lost.

    Custom CSS

    For changing how the bubble, the reader page and the toolbar popup look with CSS rules of your own. This page stays out of their reach, so a wrong rule can always be undone here: clear the field and save.

    Example: .bubble { outline: 3px solid darkorange; } draws an orange line around every bubble.

    More about the CSS rules and the names

    The rules apply inside the translation bubble, on the reader page - its bar and lists included - and in the toolbar button's window, never on the pages you read. A wrong rule can break the look or the controls of those places until the field is cleared.

    Rules that would load anything from the network (url(), @import, @font-face) are refused. The names of the elements are kept from version to version where possible; when one has to change, the release notes say so. The names come from these files:

    tooltip.js (the bubble) reader.css (the reader) popup.css (the popup) page.css (shared by every page)

    About re/read

    Everything re/read does happens on this device (offline): translating, the dictionary, the reading list, your saved phrases. Nothing you read or select is ever sent anywhere. An internet connection is needed only when you want to download a new translation model or a dictionary - and only from the two URLs shown openly in those sections.
