First steps

re/read needs something to explain words with: a translation model or a dictionary. Either one is enough; the other can come later.

  • Download a translation model or a dictionary Translation models Dictionaries
  • Pin re/read to the toolbar

Last, pin re/read to the toolbar: the puzzle-piece icon at the right end of the bar, then the gear next to re/read and "Pin to Toolbar". The button opens the reader, the reading list, your saved phrases and the switch for the site you are on.

Languages

Shows the language pairs downloaded to this device. To add new languages, download them in the Translation models section below.

Without a translation model the pair sets which language you are reading and which pair your phrases are saved under. Pairs come from installed models and dictionaries.

Translation models

Engine: Bergamot, built into the extension; translation offline.

Note! A model translates sentences well, but a single word on its own is often translated wrongly - the result is cut short, or is a word that does not exist. For single words the better source is a dictionary - the Dictionaries section below.

A translation model is downloaded separately for each pair of languages. These are official Mozilla files; the translating happens entirely on this device.

Add a model from files

To add by hand a model the list above does not offer, load its files here.

Pick the three files of one direction and they are stored locally. Gzipped files are unpacked here, so the downloads can be used as they come:

  • model.enpl.intgemm.alphas.bin
  • lex.50.50.enpl.s2t.bin
  • vocab.enpl.spm

Adding a model from files makes no network request, and this page is the way in on the day the server the models come from stops working.

With this on, the bubble still shows dictionary meanings, and you can save phrases and see their underlines. Ideal for reading with dictionaries or in your native language.

On an ordinary page nothing happens when you select text - open a page in the reader from re/read's button or with Alt+Shift+R. 

In the reader a selection is only a highlight for the moment: no bubble opens, a tap or Escape clears it, and Ctrl+C or Cmd+C copies it (on a touch screen nothing can be copied).

Dictionaries

Add dictionaries to see detailed word definitions in the translation bubble. The bubble shows the entries in the order below, language by language. The arrows change it.

Downloaded by you:

Download dictionaries

The dictionaries in the catalogue come from WikDict. You can add your own from a file or from a link, several for one language as well. The sources, each with its licence: reapps.eu/read. The list of dictionaries to download is from - - .

    Dictionary downloads come from - - where WikDict publishes the dictionaries it builds from Wiktionary (CC BY-SA). The extension connects to this public server only when you update the list or press Download. No personal data is ever sent.

    Add a dictionary from files

    Import your own dictionaries in the StarDict format. Unpack the archive, select the required files (.ifo, .idx, .dict), choose the languages and press Add dictionary.

    • name.ifo - what the dictionary calls itself
    • name.idx - the index of words
    • name.dict or name.dict.dz - the entries
    • name.syn - other spellings, when the dictionary has them

    A monolingual dictionary works too, for example English-English: choose the same language on both sides.

    Adding from files makes no network request. Any StarDict files work, including the catalogue's own, which are published at download.wikdict.com/dictionaries/stardict.

    Add a dictionary from a link

    Paste the address of a StarDict dictionary packed as a .zip file, choose the languages and press Download. The extension fetches the archive from the link, unpacks it and checks it on this device, exactly like files you pick yourself. Only https addresses are accepted. An archive that is not a .zip file (.tar.xz, .tar.zst) has to be unpacked and added from files.

    You can find links to dictionaries at reapps.eu/read.

    Bubble and phrases

    On an ordinary page, selecting text brings up a bubble with two buttons instead of the translation bubble: open the page in the reader, and go to the reading list.

    Bubble

    Shows the dictionary entries and the translated sentence, with no press on “More”. Worth keeping on when you have a dictionary: a model's translation of a single word is often wrong, while a dictionary gives the exact definitions. The bubble over a phrase you saved earlier shows only the saved translation - the sentence and the dictionary entries appear after a press on “More”.

    Bubble text size

    100%

    Adjusts the text and button size inside the translation bubble across all pages.

    Saved phrases

    Saving a phrase saves the sentence it stood in as well. 

    The sentence is then shown under the phrase on the saved phrases page. If a phrase was saved earlier without a sentence, the sentence around it is saved the next time you open its bubble on a page. Like the phrase, the sentence is kept only on this device. Switched off, no new sentence is saved, but the ones saved earlier are not deleted.

    Save "read", and "reads" and "reading" are underlined too. 

    The bubble over them shows what you saved for "read". Which forms count comes from your dictionary for the language you read, so one has to be installed. For now only when you read English. Switched off, only the exact form you saved is underlined.

    Reading view

    A single page can still be saved in the reader with “Save to reading list”, or taken out of the list with “Delete”. 

    Your saved pages, highlights and reading progress are kept safe even if the original website changes or disappears.

    Saved

    Give the name of a font installed on this device, for example Noto Serif. For re/read to use it, open the Aa panel and choose Custom in the Type row. Leave the field empty to use the default fonts.

    words a minute Saved

    Used only to estimate the reading time shown in the reading list and under an article's title. It is the number of words you read in a minute. 200 is an unhurried pace in your own language; in a language you are learning it is usually 100-150.

    Pages layout

    These work in the reading view when the Aa panel's layout is set to Pages instead of Scroll.

    The current page and how many there are, for example 3 / 12, at the foot of the window.

    A visible confirmation that the page has changed.

    Reading aloud

    In the bubble, in the lists of saved phrases and highlights, and in the reader. 

    Turning this off hides every one of them. The voice and the speed you chose are remembered and apply again when you turn it back on.

    A separate voice for each language; only voices that speak offline are offered. Speech is made on this device and nothing is sent to the internet.

    Data and copies

    Data on this device

    Space taken up by re/read's data.  

    The total is the browser's own estimate for all of re/read's data. It counts the databases' indexes and caches as well, which is why it is larger than the parts below it added up. The parts count the text and the pictures alone, without indexes: one dictionary entry takes several times more room in the database than its text alone, so Other is mostly the dictionaries' database. All of the data is in the browser's own database (IndexedDB), inside the browser's profile - not in separate files on your computer. Deleting an article or a book removes its text, pictures, highlights, notes and reading position for good.

    Backups

    re/read keeps copies of your phrases, your highlights and your reading list in the extension's own storage, apart from the database. 

    The copies are written again at every change: when you save or delete a phrase, a highlight or a page in the reading list. They protect the data when the browser clears its storage or when a database goes wrong. The database is IndexedDB and the copies are in the extension's storage (storage.local) - both inside the browser's profile, not in files on your computer.

    Reading list

    The reading list then takes twice the space. Copies of your phrases and highlights are always made.

    Uninstalling re/read deletes all of its data, the copies with it. Before you uninstall it, or before you move your data to another device, save a copy as a file: Export in writes one reread-backup.zip with the reading list, your phrases, your highlights and the settings. Models and dictionaries are not in it - they can be downloaded again. The export on the saved phrases page is a different file: the phrases alone, as TSV, for Anki or the KOReader plugin.

    Switched-off sites

    On these sites re/read does not translate and shows no bubble and no underlines. Add a site in the re/read window in the toolbar, or by typing its address below. 

    There is no switch in that window when both Use without a translation model and Do not show the bubble when selecting text are on: nothing happens on ordinary pages then, so there is nothing to switch off.

    The address as the browser shows it, for example example.org; www.example.org is the same site. A subdomain such as news.example.org is a separate site. A page's whole address can be pasted; only the site part is kept.

    Custom CSS

    Your own CSS rules change how the bubble, the reading view and the re/read window look. This page is not subject to those rules, so a wrong rule can always be undone here: clear the field and save.

    Example: .bubble { outline: 3px solid darkorange; } draws an orange line around every bubble.

    More about the CSS rules and the names

    The rules apply inside the translation bubble, on the reader page - its bar and lists included - and in the toolbar button's window, never on the pages you read. A wrong rule can break the look or the controls of those places until the field is cleared.

    Rules that would load anything from the network (url(), @import, @font-face) are refused. The names of the elements are kept from version to version where possible; when one has to change, it is written in the release notes. The names come from these files:

    tooltip.js (the bubble) reader.css (the reader) popup.css (the popup) page.css (shared by every page)

    About re/read

    Everything re/read does happens on this device (offline): translating, the dictionary, the reading list, your saved phrases. Nothing you read or select is ever sent anywhere. An internet connection is needed only to download a new translation model or a dictionary - and only from the two addresses given in the Translation models and Dictionaries sections.
